# AI Pulse

A free, non-commercial, worldwide briefing on AI. It reads 200+ public sources, keeps only AI stories, sorts them into
six streams and links every card to the original.

**Live site:** https://projectaipulse.com/ · **Version 2** (3 October 2026)

## Streams

| Stream | What's in it | Updated |
|---|---|---|
| **Releases** | New models, products and open-source launches | Labs every 30 min; the rest every 6 h |
| **Industry** | Company news, deals, AI incidents, studies, opinion | Every 6 h |
| **Research** | Papers only | Every 6 h |
| **Regulation tracker** | AI proposals, laws, bodies and standards, by country | Every 6 h |
| **Policy** | What governments, courts and politicians do about AI | Every 6 h |
| **Infra** | AI's data centres and their power, water, land and emissions | Every 6 h |

## Features

- **Front page:** the latest stories, Today in AI ranked by how widely each story is reported, what people are discussing, the week's story, word and meme, the newest monthly issues and the topics most in the news.
- **Six streams**, each with its own page, filters and search back to 2023.
- **Pages within the streams:** Industry by kind of story (opinion, company blogs, studies, AI incidents, tutorials, events), Releases by company (a page per big AI lab), Policy by who is speaking (governments, rights and advocacy groups, research institutes, AI companies, news reports), Research by subject (read from each paper's title) and Infra by subject (power, water, emissions, local pushback, builds and deals, rules and permits, hardware).
- **One card per event**, in English (stories in other languages are translated offline).
- **Email**, daily or weekly, with the streams and topics you pick.
- **Listen:** a two-minute audio digest at the top of each email: the day's top six, one per stream (your own six if you picked streams or topics); on Sunday, the week's important updates first. Read by an open-source voice run offline, kept on the site for 7 days.
- **Weekly dossier:** your own questions, answered with the week's stories.
- **[AI laws by country](https://projectaipulse.com/tracker/)** with each bill's stage; every place's **[AI laws adopted](https://projectaipulse.com/tracker/laws-adopted/)**, **[AI bills](https://projectaipulse.com/tracker/bills/)** and **[AI bodies](https://projectaipulse.com/tracker/ai-bodies/)** on a page each, as tables filterable by place, stage, source and month; and **[AI standards](https://projectaipulse.com/standards/)**.
- **[Topics](https://projectaipulse.com/topics/):** storylines followed over time, news and laws on one timeline.
- **[The month in AI](https://projectaipulse.com/monthly/):** a booklet for every month since January 2026, with the month's biggest stories, the rules that moved, a timeline and each stream's highlights, plus a page of insights for each month (how each stream's share compares with the past year, what made the busiest days, the money moves (funding rounds by theme, a valuation ladder, the big acquisitions and compute deals, read from the amounts the headlines give), US vs China (papers and open-weight models on Hugging Face), legal and risk (lawsuits, regulators and AI incidents by company), and the companies, themes and places in the news). Its summary is written offline by an open-source model and checked against the month's headlines.
- **[Glossary](https://projectaipulse.com/glossary/):** AI words in plain English, each with its newest story.
- **Saved stories:** a bookmark on each story, kept in your browser only.
- **Dark mode** and **RSS**: one feed per stream, or [all at once](https://projectaipulse.com/feeds/all.opml).
- **Free, no ads, no cookies.**

### Regulation

- **[Rule echoes](https://projectaipulse.com/rule-echoes/):** the subjects AI laws keep coming back to, and the places that took each one up, in order, from the tracker's official records.
- **[Harm → rule](https://projectaipulse.com/harm-to-rule/):** AI harms the AI Incident Database logged since 2023, by subject, beside the tracker's rules on the same subject; a rule that follows harms isn't shown to be caused by them.
- **[AI in court](https://projectaipulse.com/ai-in-court/):** lawsuits against AI makers over copyright, privacy and people's likeness since 2023, in several countries: who sued whom, the country of the court, and where each case stands, step by step (filed, an early ruling, partly settled, decided, settled or thrown out), each step checked by hand against the story that reported it; a new step is added once checked, so a case's status follows the rulings.
- **[AI fines and bans](https://projectaipulse.com/ai-fines-and-bans/):** two halves, like AI pacts and orders. Bans: AI products a regulator banned, blocked or stopped since 2022 (privacy, consumer and communications regulators), and whether each was lifted. Fines: fines and settlements paid to a regulator over AI since 2022, each amount in the currency it was set in and never added up, with whether it still stands, is challenged in court or was annulled. Every row checked by hand and linked to the regulator's own notice (a news report where the regulator published none); investigations not yet decided, lawsuits by people and courts fining lawyers are left out, and what governments made AI companies do stays on AI pacts and orders.

### Industry

- **[Promised vs shipped](https://projectaipulse.com/promises/):** when an AI company says it will release something, the later headline saying it did, found by rules from headlines alone (no AI model) and shown only once checked by hand, with the day each launch was actually out.
- **[Jobs blamed on AI](https://projectaipulse.com/jobs-blamed-on-ai/):** big job cuts the news linked to AI since 2023, each checked by hand against the reason the company itself gave (it didn't say AI, AI now does the work, cuts to spend on AI, or AI took its customers); only cuts the company confirmed, each with its source.
- **[AI money](https://projectaipulse.com/who-owns-a-piece-of-whom/):** where the big money in AI is going: every funding round, compute deal and buyout of $1B or more since 1 January 2026 as a dot per deal along the year, the big companies' stakes in AI labs as a grid of investors by labs (what the story says each put in or holds, "up to" amounts marked), and the deals where the money comes back (a company invests, and the company it invested in buys its chips or cloud). Only closed rounds and signed deals, each checked by hand against its story; amounts as the stories give them, never added up.
- **[AI buyouts](https://projectaipulse.com/ai-buyouts/):** every AI company bought, merged in or hired away whole since 1 January 2026 (or any company an AI lab bought), as a shopping list per buyer (a dot per deal along the year, bigger for a bigger price) and a table that filters like Excel: buyer, company bought, price as the story gives it (marked "reported" when it came from a news report, never added up) and whether the deal is done, agreed or blocked. Each deal checked by hand against its story; talks, letters of intent and "reportedly" deals are left out.

### Releases

- **[Models being retired](https://projectaipulse.com/models-being-retired/):** every model OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Mistral have set a shut-down date for, with the replacement each lab recommends and the day it told developers, read from each lab's own deprecation page every 6 hours (robots.txt decides, as for labs' blogs); the next 30 days marked, and the past 3 months' shut-downs below.
- **[Release cards](https://projectaipulse.com/release-cards/):** every major AI model release since 1 July 2026 (one that at least two news outlets reported within three days of launch), newest first: whether its weights can be downloaded (open, weights only, or closed), the licence as the lab names it with its main limit, where it can be used (only places the lab or a story names) its price on the lab's own API at launch and its safety report (the lab's first document giving results of its safety tests on the model: on launch day, how many days later, or none found on the lab's own pages, looked for again daily until 30 days after launch); each row checked by hand against its story or lab page, the licence text and Hugging Face. `python -m aipulse releases` lists release stories not checked yet.
- **[Lab timelines](https://projectaipulse.com/lab-timelines/):** each AI lab's month since 1 July 2026 in one grid, labs down the side and months across: its major releases (from Release cards), its API price cuts and rises (an existing model's price changing, from the day it took effect), the places a model reached after launch, any model it pulled from users after launching it (marked ✕; none so far), and how many models it shut down (from Models being retired), with the next three months' scheduled shutdowns in a grey column; a lab's name opens its rows as a table, each linking to its source, and labs with a single release are collapsed under the grid. Price changes, new places and pulled models are checked by hand against their story or the lab's own page. `python -m aipulse timelines` lists stories not checked yet.
- **[The model economy](https://projectaipulse.com/model-economy/):** the cost of keeping up, in two tables. Launch prices: each new model's launch price on its lab's own API (from Release cards) against the last model in the same line, tagged cheaper, same or pricier; the earlier model's launch price is checked by hand against its story or the lab's page, and models priced per image, minute or hour are left out. How long before you must switch: for every model shutting down since 1 January 2026, the days between the lab telling developers and the shutdown, from Models being retired, with each lab's median, shortest and longest warning (Google's page gives no day it told developers). `python -m aipulse economy` lists priced releases not checked yet.

### Policy

- **[AI safety hub](https://projectaipulse.com/ai-safety-hub/):** the system cards, safety evaluations, red-teaming reports and safety frameworks that AI labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Meta), government safety institutes (UK AI Security Institute, NIST's CAISI), independent evaluators (METR, Apollo Research), the Frontier Model Forum and the International AI Safety Report published in 2026. Each was read by hand on the publisher's own pages (only where robots.txt allows), with its abstract word for word and a link to the original PDF or paper. Search them, or filter by type (system cards, evaluations, red-teaming, frameworks) and by risk (jailbreaks, prompt injection, cyber, weapons, misalignment and more).
- **[AI consultations](https://projectaipulse.com/ai-consultations/):** governments asking the public about AI: consultations whose own title names AI, open now or closed in the past 6 months, with who is asking, the closing date and the published outcome where the list links one, read every 6 hours from the official lists of the UK (GOV.UK), the US (Federal Register), the EU (Have Your Say) and Canada (its open data list of federal consultations).
- **[AI pacts and orders](https://projectaipulse.com/ai-pacts-and-orders/):** two halves. Pacts: the summit pledges on AI (Bletchley, Seoul, Paris, New Delhi) and the Council of Europe's AI treaty, with who signed each (and who ratified the treaty), as each official list gives it (GOV.UK, the Élysée, India's Press Information Bureau, the Council of Europe's treaty office), copied by hand since the lists hardly change and two of those sites refuse automated readers, and the deals governments signed with AI companies. Orders: what governments made AI companies do this year, each ban or hold drawn from start to end with any court rulings, then the orders, investigations and court cases. Every deal, ban and order checked by hand against the story that reported it; talks, reported plans and threats left out.
- **[What governments use AI for](https://projectaipulse.com/government-ai-use/):** the lists governments publish of the AI they use. Who tells you: every such list we found, the four read in full once a day (the United States' federal AI use case inventory, as OMB publishes it on GitHub; the Government of Canada AI Register; the Dutch Algorithm Register; the UK's algorithmic transparency records on GOV.UK) and the others checked by hand (Norway's KI Norge overview, Estonia's Kratid use stories, Brazil's count of federal AI solutions); lists kept by anyone other than a government (France's observatory, Chile's university repository) aren't counted. Whose AI they run: the suppliers named in the lists that name one (Microsoft, OpenAI, Google, Amazon...). Then a card per full list with three facts by its own definitions and uses that touch people, each linking to its own page with every entry and filters. Dutch entries stay in Dutch as registered.

### Research

- **[Papers that made the news](https://projectaipulse.com/papers-in-the-news/):** research papers the news reported on since 13 July 2026, each checked by hand against the paper itself (only papers on a preprint server, in a journal or at a conference), laid out as a newspaper page with a one-line plain explanation of each and the paper's code and demo links, then each subject's count beside the papers we read.
- **[New tests for AI](https://projectaipulse.com/new-tests-for-ai/):** the new benchmarks in the research papers we read since 17 July 2026, by subject: what each tests in one plain line and the best result its paper reports, with the model that got it where the paper names one, each checked by hand against the paper's whole abstract (only papers whose main point is a new test that scores others' models); the tests that give a top score as a percentage are lined up from 0 to 100. Each test shows its paper's code and demo links, and a "Beaten" note when a later paper's abstract reports a better result on the same measure (checked by hand). `python -m aipulse tests` lists new benchmark papers not checked yet; `python -m aipulse tests-beaten` lists later papers that name a test.
- **[Compute behind notable models](https://projectaipulse.com/compute-behind-notable-models/):** how much computing trained each notable AI model since 2020, measured against GPT-4, from Epoch AI's open dataset (CC BY 4.0) every 6 hours: every model with an estimate as a dot, the largest training runs from scratch and the newest estimates, each with how sure Epoch is, what it was built on and its post-training compute where Epoch gives it (a model built on another is a hollow dot and isn't ranked, as its figure includes its base model's training); models with no estimate are counted, never guessed.
- **[Who publishes AI research on Hugging Face](https://projectaipulse.com/who-publishes-ai-research/):** the year's AI papers on our Research cards that a company put out (mostly from Hugging Face's daily papers list), counted by company with where each is based and its main subject; the research subject whose share of papers is rising, month by month; and the researchers we follow who published most. Read only from the papers we already collect.

### Infra

- **[Data-centre builds](https://projectaipulse.com/data-centre-builds/):** new data centres worldwide since 15 July 2026: who is behind each, where, its size and cost as the story states them (never added up), and whether it is announced, being built or open, each checked by hand against its story.
- **[Who powers AI](https://projectaipulse.com/who-powers-ai/):** deals and plans to power data centres since 15 July 2026: who supplies the power, what it comes from (nuclear, gas, renewables, fuel cells or a utility), what it will run, and whether it is signed, planned or dropped, each checked by hand against its story.
- **[Pushback & permits](https://projectaipulse.com/pushback-and-permits/):** decisions on data centres since 15 July 2026 by governments, councils, courts and grid operators: where, who decided, and whether they approved, paused, blocked or limited them, each checked by hand against its story.
- **[Chip export rules](https://projectaipulse.com/chip-export-rules/):** what Nvidia and AMD could sell to China under each US decision since 2022, a chip per row and a decision per column (from the rules in the Federal Register, public domain, and the companies' own SEC filings), then the other moves: limits on chipmaking tools and memory, rules for the rest of the world, and China's answers (official notices from MOFCOM, SAMR, METI and the Dutch Government Gazette). Each row says whether it still stands; the few resting on a news report are footnoted.

## How we keep it legal

1. **robots.txt must allow us**, checked before every request. github.com pages are never read, only GitHub's API.
2. **Terms:** news and other sites are read only if their terms don't forbid robots or limit use to personal use. AI labs' and companies' own blogs, and news sites whose terms pages our reader can't open: robots.txt decides.
3. **No workarounds:** nothing behind a login, bot check or paywall.
4. **Only what's needed:** headline, short description, date and link.
5. **Credit and non-commercial use**, as the licences ask.
6. **No tracking of readers:** visits are only counted anonymously (GoatCounter: no cookie, IP address or ID kept); see the [privacy page](https://projectaipulse.com/privacy.html).

Every source's robots.txt and terms were audited on 7 October 2026. The evidence for each is in `aipulse/terms.py`;
`python -m aipulse audit` checks them again. Questions: projectaipulse@gmail.com.

## Run it

```bash
python -m aipulse collect     # fetch every source into aipulse.db
python -m aipulse serve       # http://127.0.0.1:8000
python -m aipulse audit       # check every source's robots.txt and terms again
python -m pytest -q           # tests (run locally)
```

Hosted on GitHub Pages (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`); the email is sent by `.github/workflows/digest.yml` and
sign-ups go to a Google Apps Script web app (`apps-script/Code.gs`). The domain (about $11 a year) is the only cost.
